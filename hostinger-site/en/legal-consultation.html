What does a legal consultation in Qatar cover?

A legal consultation is a dedicated session in which you set out the facts of your matter to a lawyer, who explains where you stand under Qatari law, what options you have and the risks attached to each. It is the right first step before you sign a contract, reply to a legal notice, file a complaint or make any decision with legal or financial consequences.

At Al Murqab Law Office, a legal consultation typically covers:

  • Understanding the facts: hearing the details of your matter, the timeline and the parties involved.
  • Reviewing documents: reading contracts, correspondence, notices and any relevant judgments or decisions.
  • Legal analysis: identifying the applicable law, such as the Civil Code, the Labour Law or the Family Law, and the court or committee with jurisdiction.
  • Assessing your options: amicable settlement, a formal notice, a specialist committee, litigation or arbitration.
  • Critical deadlines: flagging time limits that, if missed, could weaken your position or cost you your right.
  • Next steps: a clear, practical plan for what to do now and what to avoid.

A legal consultation does not necessarily mean going to court. Many matters are resolved by the right preventive step or a well-considered reply sent at the right time.

Urgent legal consultations, 24/7

Some situations cannot wait until office hours, which run Sunday to Thursday. That is why urgent legal consultations are available 24 hours a day, seven days a week. Typical urgent situations include:

Documents to prepare for your legal consultation, by case type

The better organised your documents, the more precise the legal advice. Do not worry if you do not have everything; we will tell you what is missing and how to obtain it.

Documents for every consultation

  • A copy of your Qatar ID or passport, or the commercial registration if the client is a company.
  • A short written summary of the facts in date order.
  • Correspondence, messages and notices exchanged with the other party.

Confidential legal advice in Arabic or English

Everything you share with us, facts and documents alike, is treated with complete professional confidentiality, whether or not you go on to instruct the firm. Confidentiality is not an extra; it is a core duty of the legal profession.

For residents and expatriates

We advise in both Arabic and English and explain procedures and legal terms in plain language. This matters to expatriates in particular, because Arabic is the language of Qatar's national courts, and many people need someone to explain each step and each document before they sign it.

For companies and business owners

We advise managers, partners and HR teams and take care to protect commercially sensitive information. If your company's legal needs are recurring, an ongoing legal counsel arrangement for companies may suit you better than consulting matter by matter.

If you are outside Qatar

You can consult us remotely by phone, video or WhatsApp. If your matter requires someone to represent you before a court or authority, we will explain what is needed for a power of attorney in Qatar.

What happens after the legal consultation?

You leave the consultation knowing where you stand and what comes next. Depending on your matter, the path may be one of the following:

  • Advice only: where guidance is enough for you to act on your own.
  • A written legal opinion: for matters that need to be documented, particularly for companies.
  • Pre-litigation steps: a formal notice, negotiation or a written amicable settlement.
  • The competent committee: some disputes must first go to a specialist body, such as the Labour Dispute Settlement Committees or the Rental Disputes Settlement Committee.
  • Court representation or arbitration: under a clear, written fee agreement before work begins. To understand how a case progresses, see our guide to litigation in the Qatari courts.

Watch the deadlines

Some appeal and grievance periods are short. For example, an administrative decision must generally be challenged within 60 days. Do not leave your consultation until a deadline is about to expire.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a legal consultation cost in Qatar?

+

Consultation fees depend on the nature and complexity of the matter and the volume of documents to be reviewed. At Al Murqab Law Office we explain the fee and agree it with you in advance, and no further work begins without a new, clear agreement.

Can I get legal advice via WhatsApp in Qatar?

+

Yes. You can contact the firm on WhatsApp to outline your matter and send photos of your documents, and we will agree the most suitable consultation format with you. For complex matters we may suggest a video call or an in-person meeting to review the full file.

Do you offer legal consultations in English?

+

Yes. We advise citizens, expatriates and companies in both Arabic and English. We also explain documents and procedures written in Arabic so you understand what you are signing and its consequences.

Is what I tell you in a consultation confidential?

+

Yes. All information and documents you share are treated with complete professional confidentiality. This applies whether or not you instruct the firm after the consultation.

Can I get an urgent legal consultation outside office hours?

+

Office hours are Sunday to Thursday, 8:00 to 17:00, and urgent consultations are available 24/7. Typical urgent situations include an arrest, a summons for questioning, a travel ban or a legal deadline that is about to expire.

Can I consult a lawyer in Qatar while I am abroad?

+

Yes. Remote consultations are available by phone, video or WhatsApp. If your matter requires representation before a court or authority in Qatar, we will explain what is needed to issue a power of attorney.

Do I need a lawyer to file a labour or rental complaint?

+

In many cases you can file the complaint yourself, but advice beforehand helps you define your claims precisely, prepare your documents and meet the deadlines. Choosing the wrong forum or framing the request poorly can delay the outcome.

What is the difference between a consultation and instructing a lawyer?

+

A consultation assesses your legal position and sets out your options, while instructing a lawyer means the lawyer represents you and takes action on your behalf. You can start with a consultation and then decide whether you need representation.
