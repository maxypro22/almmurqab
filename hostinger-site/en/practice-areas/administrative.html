You need an administrative law lawyer in Qatar when a ministry, government body, public authority or public institution issues a decision that affects your legal position: termination of service, denial of promotion, a disciplinary penalty, refusal of a licence, or withdrawal of an approval previously granted. These disputes differ from civil cases in one crucial respect. The deadline for an annulment claim is short, and it runs from the date the decision was published, notified to you or came to your certain knowledge, not from the date you decide to act.

At Al Murqab Law Office we start by establishing the nature of the decision. Is it a final administrative decision? Does it fall within the Administrative Circuit's jurisdiction, or is it among the decisions the law excludes? Must a grievance be filed before going to court? We then draft the grievance or statement of claim on the grounds of challenge recognised by law, seek suspension of the decision where the conditions are met, and claim compensation where the decision has caused loss.

Our guide to appealing administrative decisions in Qatar explains the steps in plain terms, and you may also find our guide to litigation before the Qatari courts helpful. If your dispute concerns employment with a private-sector employer, it will usually belong under labour law rather than administrative justice. We work in Arabic and English, in complete confidence.

The legal framework for administrative justice in Qatar

Law No. 7 of 2007 on the settlement of administrative disputes, as amended by Law No. 12 of 2013 and Law No. 15 of 2019, sets out the jurisdiction and procedures of Qatar's administrative courts. It established one or more Administrative Circuits of three judges within the Court of First Instance, with exclusive jurisdiction over the administrative disputes it defines, and an Administrative Appeal Circuit within the Court of Appeal. Where the law is silent, the Civil and Commercial Procedures Law and Law No. 12 of 2005 on cassation challenges in non-criminal matters apply (Article 11).

What the Administrative Circuit hears

  • Disputes over salaries, pensions, bonuses and allowances due to employees or their heirs.
  • Applications to annul final administrative decisions on the promotion of employees at first grade and below or equivalent, or on termination of their service, and disciplinary decisions concerning them.
  • Applications by individuals and legal entities to annul final administrative decisions, subject to exceptions set by law.
  • Claims for compensation for these decisions, whether brought on their own or alongside an annulment claim.
  • Administrative contract disputes.

Decisions excluded from the Administrative Circuit

Article 3 excludes a range of decisions, including Amiri orders, decisions and decrees; decisions under the laws on entry, exit, residence and deportation of expatriates and on permanent residency; expropriation for public benefit; weapons and ammunition licences; private associations and foundations; and printing and publishing. Checking whether a decision can be challenged is therefore always the first step.

Grounds for annulment

An annulment application is admissible only if based on one of these grounds: lack of competence, a defect of form, breach of laws and regulations or error in applying or interpreting them, or abuse of power. An authority's refusal or failure to take a decision it was required to take under laws and regulations is treated as an administrative decision (Article 4).

Filing an annulment claim does not automatically suspend the decision. However, where requested in the statement of claim, the Administrative Circuit may suspend certain decisions if the challenge appears serious and the effects of implementation would be difficult to reverse, or may order continued payment of all or part of the salary pending a ruling on a termination decision (Article 7). Annulment judgments are binding on everyone (Article 11).

Article 10 requires any grievance stages set by other applicable laws to be exhausted before a decision can be challenged for annulment or compensation. Government employees should also check the Civil Human Resources Law promulgated by Law No. 15 of 2016, as amended.

What to prepare before contacting an administrative law lawyer in Qatar

  • A copy of the decision you want to challenge, and proof of when it was published, notified to you or came to your knowledge.
  • A copy of any grievance you filed, proof of its filing date, and any response.
  • Documents establishing your legal position: appointment decision, performance reports, earlier licence and correspondence with the authority.
  • A statement of any financial entitlements in dispute.
  • A short written summary of why you believe the decision is unlawful.

Frequently asked questions

What is the deadline to challenge an administrative decision in Qatar?

+

An annulment claim must be filed within sixty days of the decision's publication in the Official Gazette or the authority's bulletins, its notification to the person concerned, or proof of their certain knowledge of it, under Article 6 of Law No. 7 of 2007. The deadline is interrupted by filing a grievance with the authority. You should therefore act as soon as you learn of the decision.

Must I file a grievance before going to the administrative court?

+

A grievance is a precondition for employees' claims to annul final decisions on promotion, termination of service and disciplinary matters, and such claims are inadmissible until the grievance has been filed and the period for deciding it has passed (Article 5). Article 10 also requires any grievance stages set by other laws to be exhausted. In other cases, a grievance still interrupts the deadline for filing a claim.

What if the authority does not respond to my grievance?

+

The authority must decide the grievance within sixty days of its filing, and any rejection must give reasons. If sixty days pass without a response, the grievance is treated as rejected, and the deadline for filing a claim runs from the date of the express or implied rejection.

Does filing a claim suspend the administrative decision?

+

No. An annulment claim does not automatically suspend the decision. If requested in the statement of claim, the Administrative Circuit may suspend the decision where the challenge appears serious and implementation would cause effects that are hard to reverse, or may order continued salary payments in termination cases (Article 7).

Can residency or deportation decisions be challenged before the Administrative Circuit?

+

Article 3 of Law No. 7 of 2007 excludes from the Administrative Circuit's jurisdiction decisions made under the laws on entry, exit, residence and deportation of expatriates, together with other decisions such as Amiri orders and decrees and expropriation for public benefit. An annulment claim against such decisions is therefore not admissible before the Administrative Circuit, and it is advisable to take legal advice on the options available in each case.

Can I claim compensation for an unlawful administrative decision?

+

Yes. The Administrative Circuit hears claims for compensation for administrative decisions within its jurisdiction, whether brought on their own or alongside an annulment claim (Article 3). The claimant must show that the decision was unlawful, the harm it caused, and the causal link between them.
